GaiaSeis: licensing note

Panagiotis Elias, Deforming Gaia Lab, Department of Geology, University of Patras. Prepared 2026-10-29, final decision recorded 2026-10-30, for the public release 0.5.0 (GitHub and Zenodo). This is a technical summary of the choice and its reasons, not legal advice.

1. Summary

Decision: the GaiaSeis software (the platform, its engine interface package GaiaSeisEngineAPI, tools, tests and examples) and the GaiaVario engine (GaiaVario.jl, the clean-room variometric engine that is the default engine of GaiaSeis) are released under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 only (SPDX: AGPL-3.0-only). The documents (user manual, reports, leaflet, guides, figures) are released under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 (CC BY-NC 4.0). The translated engine PPPHVA.jl is not distributed until the PPPH-VA authors answer (sections 6 and 7); the platform runs without it.

Open point: the licence of the original MATLAB PPPH-VA code, from which PPPHVA.jl is translated, is not stated anywhere. The authors have to be asked; their answer decides the licence of PPPHVA.jl (section 6). It does not affect the release: GaiaSeis 0.5.0 ships with GaiaVario and offers the PPPHVA path only where that package is installed separately.

3. The NonCommercial (NC) clause

  • Definition (CC BY-NC 4.0): NonCommercial means "not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation". Exchanging copies for no payment is not commercial.
  • It is not open source. The Open Source Definition and the Free Software Definition forbid restrictions on fields of use. Software under NC cannot be called open source or free software.
  • It is incompatible with the GPL family. GPL/AGPL forbid adding restrictions. Code under NC cannot be combined with GPL/AGPL code in one distributed work. Using NC for the software would also block anyone from combining GaiaSeis with GPL components.
  • Vague boundary. Is use by a company's research group, a contractor of a seismological service, or a university project with industrial funding "commercial"? The text leaves this to interpretation, which deters adopters and creates dispute risk.
  • Creative Commons itself does not recommend its licences for software (they do not address patents, source-code distribution or compilation). It recommends software licences such as those of the FSF or OSI.
  • Funding and repositories. The EU programme (TRANSFORM2) and Zenodo favour open licences; an NC software licence would be at odds with open-science expectations for publicly funded software.
  • Where NC is reasonable: for texts, figures and manuals. The author keeps the right to commercial reuse of the documents, and the documents carry no code-compatibility problem.

4. What is more appropriate

For the software, a copyleft open-source licence is more appropriate than NC. Reasons: NC cannot be combined with GPL/AGPL libraries, is not recognised as open source, is ambiguous, and is discouraged by Creative Commons for code. The sensible choice is between permissive (MIT) and copyleft (GPL or AGPL).

  • MIT maximises adoption but lets a third party take GaiaSeis, improve it and keep the improvements closed.
  • GPL-3.0 keeps distributed derivatives open, but a company could run a modified GaiaSeis as a hosted service without releasing changes.
  • AGPL-3.0 closes that gap, because GaiaSeis is itself a network service (web page, SeedLink through ringserver). It keeps every public deployment's improvements available to the community, while still allowing commercial use and consultancy. It is the closest legal substitute for what NC was meant to achieve (preventing closed commercial appropriation) without the incompatibilities.

For the documents, CC BY-NC 4.0 is appropriate (or CC BY 4.0 if maximal reuse is wanted). Because documents are not code, the incompatibility and tooling objections do not apply.

Points to know about the choice

  • The author stays the copyright holder and is not bound by AGPL for his own code. A separate commercial licence (dual licensing) may be offered later, provided every contributor has agreed to it. CONTRIBUTING.md asks contributors to license their contributions under AGPL-3.0-only and to certify authorship (sign-off).
  • Anyone's use of GaiaSeis output (displacement series, miniSEED, SeedLink streams) is not covered by AGPL; the licence applies to the program, not to the data it produces.
  • Dependencies. The Julia packages used (HTTP, Gtk4 and others) carry MIT or LGPL terms and are compatible with AGPL-3.0. ringserver (EarthScope) is an external program, not linked or bundled.
  • Citation. Licence conditions require keeping the copyright and licence notice. Academic credit is requested through CITATION.cff and the Zenodo DOI.

6. The PPPH-VA question

PPPHVA.jl is a Julia translation of PPPH-VA (Bahadur, Bezcioglu, Yigit, GPS Solutions, 2023, doi 10.1007/s10291-023-01560-z). The paper calls it open-source but names no licence, and the source archive carries none. A translation is a derivative work. Without a licence, the authors' permission is required before publication.

7. Distributing PPPHVA.jl (source or binary)

A compiled or bundled PPPHVA.jl is still a derivative work of the MATLAB code, and giving a copy to someone is distribution in either form. With no licence stated, all rights remain with the authors, so permission is needed for source, binary bundle, GitHub and Zenodo alike. A GitHub or Zenodo publication cannot be reliably withdrawn.

Decision: PPPHVA.jl is not supplied in any form until the authors agree in writing. The public repository, the Linux and Windows bundles and the Zenodo deposit contain neither the package nor its data files; the platform's own dynamic-struct type replaces the one it used to import, the engine name ppphva is offered only where the package is found at load time (environment, GAIASEIS_PPPHVA_PATH, or ../PPPH_VA/PPPHVA.jl), and a profile that asks for it elsewhere fails to start with a message that says so. PPPH-VA is acknowledged in NOTICE.md and the manual as the reference implementation used during development, with its citation.

When binaries are distributed: keep the licence texts in the bundle, link the exact tagged source (AGPL source offer), and list the licences of bundled third-party software (Julia runtime and packages mostly MIT; GTK libraries LGPL). Earlier private copies of the translation given to collaborators for testing are to be mentioned in the request to the authors.

8. Why a single licence

A dual licence "AGPL-3.0 or EUPL-1.2, at the recipient's choice" was considered because EUPL-1.2 is the Commission's recommended licence for EU-funded software. A single licence, AGPL-3.0-only, was chosen instead:

  • One text to read. Recipients, contributors and repository tooling deal with one licence; the LICENSE file is the AGPL text itself, not a statement about a choice between two.
  • AGPL is already EUPL-compatible for inbound reuse. EUPL-1.2 lists AGPL-3.0 among its compatible licences, so an EUPL project may incorporate GaiaSeis code; nothing is lost on the EU side by not offering EUPL as a second option.
  • Simpler CITATION and SPDX. CITATION.cff, Project.toml, the Zenodo record and every SPDX-License-Identifier carry the plain expression AGPL-3.0-only; contributors agree to one licence and no dual-grant clause is needed.
  • Decision authority. The author, as coordinator of the TRANSFORM2 project, publishes the software under this licence without a separate technology-transfer consultation; the choice is an open copyleft licence consistent with the project's open-science obligations.
